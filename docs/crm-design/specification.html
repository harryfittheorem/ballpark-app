Clubhouse CRM

Leadership design and operating contract

Design for review · November 20, 2026 · Sample data only

Clubhouse is a family-aware operating CRM for Infinite Hitting locations. It brings adult/guardian conversations, athlete context, follow-ups, opportunities and configurable workflows into one experience. HighLevel supplies a familiar baseline; shared-inbox teamwork, linked records and explainable automation inform the proposed improvements.

This package is a proposal, not an approved launch commitment. The interactive prototype performs local simulations only. No channel is connected, no message is sent and no production data is altered. “Essential” below means essential design coverage and recommended implementation priority, not already available software.

1. What is new

CapabilityWhat staff will be able to do after approved implementation
Shared conversationsReceive and send approved adult/guardian SMS from the location identity, with accountable ownership, internal collaboration and delivery evidence.
Family-aware contactsWork with one linked family, authorized adults and separate athletes instead of duplicating a household for every sales activity.
Tags and audiencesManually or automatically apply scoped tags, build dynamic audiences, preview eligible adults and explain exclusions.
OpportunitiesManage multiple athlete/program opportunities through configurable stages with next steps and stage history.
Visual workflowsCompose triggers, filters, waits, branches, messages, tasks, tag changes and exits; simulate and approve versions before activation.
Location connectionsManage a separate Twilio SMS connector per location; optionally connect a business-owned Gmail/Workspace mailbox after feasibility approval.
Operational reportingSee human response, pipeline movement, message outcomes, blocked automation and usage without invented attribution.
Assistance and governanceReview sourced AI drafts, team collision warnings and corporate version proposals without surrendering permission or publication control.

Not included: an entire HighLevel website/funnel/store/ads platform, unlimited vendor parity, autonomous AI sending, direct coach-to-player messaging, automatic billing changes or a new native mobile app. Phone designs are responsive staff portal views.

Navigation preserves the selected location. Search results remain permission-scoped. Opening a contact from a thread keeps a route back to that thread; opening a deal preserves the adult and athlete context. A location switch clears incompatible selections, drafts and cached record context rather than rendering another location's history.

Desktop prioritizes simultaneous context and rapid operation. Phone prioritizes queue → thread → context, with a clear back path and reachable actions. Opportunities support stage selection/list movement with explicit confirmation, not drag-and-drop only. No core operation should require hover.

Keyboard: visible focus, meaningful control labels, logical order, Enter/Space activation, Escape to close dialogs, restored focus on close and errors associated with fields. Production acceptance must include screen-reader testing, contrast and reduced motion. The prototype is not an accessibility certification.

4. Identity, thread boundaries and conversion

ConceptMeaning and invariant
FamilyShared household relationship, not automatic permission to see every adult's communications.
Adult/guardianA canonical adult identity with explicit authorized athlete links and channel endpoints. Shared email/phone alone never proves identity or custody authority.
AthleteSeparate person/context; interests and program history do not automatically apply to siblings. Not a direct CRM messaging recipient.
OpportunityA location-scoped athlete/program pursuit. A family can have multiple legitimate open opportunities.
ConversationAuthorized adult participants + location/business sender + channel endpoint identity. A family summary may reference allowed threads without merging private conversations.
Membership/paymentAuthoritative operational source. CRM tags cannot redefine enrollment, arrears, consent or balances.
Prospect conversionLink or create the canonical family/adult/athlete after review, retain original lead source, activity, assignments, tags and opportunity IDs, and record conversion actor/time.

Duplicate suggestions explain matching evidence and conflicting relationships. Staff choose “keep separate,” “link existing after verification,” or request authorized review. No automatic merge by phone/email, no global merge outside a user's scope and no history deletion. Shared endpoints with uncertain adult ownership block sending until a verified recipient is chosen.

Attachments have type, size, scan/access policy and channel capability gates. SMS attachments are MMS only where sender/provider/registration supports them; unsupported files require another approved route with explicit user selection. Email capability is not assumed.

6. Conversation behavior

Queues include needs reply, mine, unassigned, follow-up due and delivery attention. Unread is staff read state, not customer SMS read evidence. Each thread identifies recipient adult, linked athlete context, location sender, channel, assignee, consent purpose and last activity.

Event/controlProposed behavior
ReplyPreview resolved recipient and sender; preserve draft. Recheck identity, role, location, endpoint, purpose, consent, suppression, ownership and caps immediately before enqueue/send.
Internal note / mentionVisually distinct mode and history; never render in outbound message content or AI customer draft context unless explicitly permitted and safely transformed. Mention notifications remain staff-only.
Teammate composingInformational presence; not a lock guarantee. A new message/assignment revision after drafting produces a stale warning and requires reread/review before send.
Assign / escalateSingle accountable owner plus optional team; record old/new owner and reason. Escalation creates a due next action.
Human takeoverPause the relevant relationship/journey's outward automation under explicit scope; show what remains queued and when resumption requires review.
ScheduleShow recipient-local and location-local time, purpose, sender, quiet-hour/cap eligibility and queued policy. Future eligibility is not guaranteed by today's preview.
Incoming replyAppend provider event once, update queue/owner notification and evaluate reply-wait/human-handoff rules. An ambiguous shared endpoint goes to review, not an arbitrary adult.
AI draftEditable proposal with source messages/timestamps and “last generated”; never sends itself and never overwrites a human draft without consent.
FailurePreserve draft and explain blocked/delayed/failed/unknown. Do not mark a provider acceptance as delivered.

SMS state vocabulary: draft → scheduled/queued → attempted → provider accepted → delivered, or blocked/skipped/failed/unknown. Out-of-order/duplicate provider events must not regress confirmed delivery; retain evidence and event timestamps. Delivery is not proof a guardian read the text.

Email is visibly optional/proposed. Calls and broad campaigns are later scope. Channel failure never silently changes provider, location sender or communication channel.

7. Tags, audiences and governance

Tags have stable identifiers, display name, category, optional color, description, entity type, location visibility and archived state. Membership, sport, consent and billing remain canonical fields rather than shadow truth in tags.

OperationRequired behavior
Manual apply/removeChoose family, adult or athlete explicitly. Record actor/time/source and only emit an event on a real state change.
Bulk editRequires separate permission. Preview affected entity count and samples, inaccessible exclusions and add/remove changes; confirm before execution. Partial failures must report per-record status in production.
RenameStable references survive. Preview dependent saved views, audiences and workflow versions; update display name, not meaning silently.
ArchivePrevent new use; show affected references and require replacement or explicit disabling/revalidation. Retain history; never silently break active audiences.
Workflow tag actionIdempotent add/remove; include source run and causal chain; prevent same-chain loops and duplicate enrollment.
Manual/workflow conflictProposed manual suppression/tombstone for that journey/version prevents immediate automated re-add until reviewed or an approved reset event occurs. Show override reason; final policy needs approval.

Audience expressions support nested AND/OR groups, has/does-not-have, any/all tag sets and authoritative fields such as location or membership status. Tags belong to the selected entity: athlete interest resolves through verified guardian links; it does not copy to every family member.

Preview shows matched entities, eligible adults, excluded adults with reasons and endpoint-level deduplication. For example, two interested siblings may produce two athlete matches but one guardian camp-marketing message with approved combined context. A distinct appointment reminder remains separate when its appointment intent differs. Household collisions are keyed by approved communication intent and time window, not simply family ID.

Enrollment uses an auditable eligibility snapshot; live state is re-evaluated at execution. Removal of an inclusion tag or addition of an exclusion tag after scheduling can suppress the queued message. Tag changes never create consent.

Example: add “Interested in Summer Camp” to an athlete → approved tag-triggered journey checks adult eligibility → preview camp draft → wait for reply or booking → reply goes to staff; authoritative booking exits nurture → optional tag update. Wording, timing and scope remain proposals.

8. Opportunity contract

The clickable prototype uses this recommended sample pipeline: New inquiry → Needs follow-up → Evaluation booked → Proposal → Won. Leadership may choose separate Evaluated or Decision pending stages rather than using Proposal. Lost/disqualified is a status with a reason, not a history-deleting action. These names are not approved sales policy.

Each opportunity shows location, family, athlete/program, stage, open/won/lost status, assigned owner, proposed value/currency, next action/due date, source, linked threads and stage history. Value is a sales estimate, not a charge or recognized revenue.

Transition/configurationValidation and recovery
Evaluation bookedRequire a real appointment reference or approved exception with reason. Changing a display stage alone cannot create a booking.
Evaluated / proposal preparationRequire completion evidence and next action as approved by leadership; exact stage mapping remains open.
WonLink verified enrollment or mark pending reconciliation; do not invent membership or trigger a charge.
Lost/disqualifiedRequire approved reason; retain history and handle related journeys explicitly.
Move backwardPreserve timestamps/history and re-evaluate workflow re-entry; not automatic replay of all messages.
Remove a stageShow affected open/closed counts, choose compatible destination, preview required-field conflicts, confirm migration and retain historic stage labels. Block deletion while unresolved.

Multiple sibling/program deals may coexist. Warn about probable duplicates but never force all family opportunities into one record. Stage ordering and required fields are versioned configuration; changes cannot silently invalidate in-flight records.

10. Workflow operating semantics

ConcernProposed contract
Enrollment identityRun = location + workflow/version + context kind/ID + triggering event ID. Retried event delivery cannot create another run.
Re-entryExplicit never / after completed / after approved cooldown. Concurrent runs for the same subject/intent are blocked by default; sibling appointment contexts remain distinct. Final intervals require approval.
Tag loopsNo event for a no-op; record causal chain; disallow same-chain reflexive re-entry and enforce bounded transitions. Legitimate later manual changes remain distinguishable.
EligibilityEntry snapshot explains why enrolled; each action checks current authoritative eligibility and approved exclusions.
Quiet hoursUse verified recipient timezone with location timezone displayed. Unknown timezone blocks or queues for manual review; never silently guesses a send time.
CapsEnforce location-business + adult endpoint global cap and per-channel/purpose cap across all workflows/manual campaigns. Policy may exempt defined service messages only after approval. Counts, windows and exemptions are open.
Priority/collisionStaff reply/human takeover supersedes incompatible nurture. Related household sends deduplicate by intent; do not suppress distinct athlete appointments.
Missed timeNode-specific skip, move to next permitted window or staff review; never burst-send all overdue reminders after recovery.
Reschedule/date changeRecompute future relative waits from current authoritative appointment/payment date, cancel obsolete queued intent and explain old/new schedule. Never resend an already completed intent without explicit policy.
RecurrenceExplicit timezone, start/end boundaries, max/re-entry policy and overlap prevention. No infinite loop hidden in a recurring trigger.
Consent/opt-outExecution-time channel/purpose/business-identity check. Incoming STOP or revocation suppresses queued affected sends, with a visible audit reason.
Lost connectionPause affected dispatch; expose queue age and reconnect action. No silent sender/channel fallback.

Simulation, publication and pausing

Simulation never calls a delivery provider, writes operational tags, creates real tasks or changes bookings. It displays sample/selected context, resolved adult and sender, filled template variables, both timezones, branch reasons, suppressed steps, tag effects and predicted timing. The prototype simulation is illustrative and is not a production policy evaluator.

Any future live test is a separate action with explicit warning, a staff-only allowlist, approved permission and real-send confirmation. It cannot be substituted silently for simulation.

Publication review shows validation results, previous/proposed version diff, approver role, assumptions, channel readiness, consent policy reference and treatment of current runs. Proposed default: existing runs remain pinned to their published version; only future entries use the new version. Migrating active runs requires explicit affected-run preview and approval. Disabling unsafe versions may cancel queued actions under an audited safety operation.

Pause offers distinct scopes: stop new enrollments; stop queued outward sends; both. State exactly whether internal tasks continue. Resumption rechecks timing, eligibility, caps and connection state; expired reminders do not flush in a burst.

Failure center distinguishes blocked, delayed, skipped, failed and unknown. Each item includes cause, safe next action, run/context/version, last evidence and permissions. Unknown delivery may be resolved only from authoritative reconciliation, not a staff guess disguised as provider proof.

Reevaluation rule

Create an internal staff reevaluation task only when there is an upcoming visit and the next payment is within ten days. Re-evaluate on relevant visit/payment-date changes and continue appropriate reminders until the task is completed. Do not substitute a thirty-day cycle. Do not automatically contact the parent or change billing. A missing visit or payment date does not satisfy the condition. Leadership must approve the exact treatment of overdue payments, due-today boundaries and “upcoming” before execution.

Each location has its own Twilio connector and sender identity. This implements the creator's proposed direction, not provisioning approval.

SettingRequired design/implementation gate

OwnershipRecord legal/business sender, number ownership, billing responsibility, administrator, portability and offboarding/revocation process.
SMS readinessConnection health, assigned number, registration/review state where applicable, approved use case, consent source, delivery callbacks and opt-out handling.
Connection actionsSecure server-mediated Connect/Reconnect/Disconnect; no raw credential fields in browser/chats, no tokens in client bundles. Prototype only simulates.
DisconnectShow affected queued sends/workflows; pause them, preserve audit and explain reconnect/offboarding. Never transfer to another location automatically.
UsageCounts/segments/provider outcomes, data freshness and budget thresholds. Show no invented per-message price or guaranteed delivery.

Twilio SMS is not SendGrid email. If SendGrid is chosen it needs separate account/domain/sender onboarding and policy. The existing system-notification sender is not replaced by this design.

Optional Gmail / Google Workspace

Recommend a business-owned location mailbox, subject to approval; not an employee's private mailbox. Connect through explicit OAuth, never mailbox passwords. Explain requested scopes and authorized mailbox. A label or application filter may constrain what Clubhouse processes but does not narrow the OAuth grant itself.

Define synchronization scope before connection: new-only versus approved bounded history, permitted threads, initial cursor and history resync behavior. Track mailbox/provider message/thread identifiers, processed events and outbound identities to deduplicate inbound, outbound and messages sent outside Clubhouse. Reply threading follows provider identifiers plus allowed adult/location context; subject lines alone do not authorize linking.

Expose last successful sync, delayed sync, revoked grant, reconnect and partial-history limitations. Revocation pauses email actions; it cannot silently route via SMS or another mailbox. Reconnect requires deliberate history/resync scope; stale cursors may require a bounded full resync with user-visible explanation.

Google sensitive/restricted scopes, OAuth verification and any applicable security assessment, Workspace administrator controls and data-use/AI restrictions must be assessed before a launch date or AI access is promised. No complete mailbox synchronization by default. Connected email is initially a correspondence proposal; bulk campaigns, transactional delivery and volume limits need a separate approved route and explicit queues.

13. Corporate templates and workflow rollout

Corporate authors a versioned proposal with change summary, scope, minimum required controls and local override policy. Locations compare their current version and overrides, simulate against their allowed context, accept or decline with reason and activate only with local authority. Report adoption status without broad customer-record access.

An edit to a shared template must not silently change a published local workflow's wording. Published runs pin template/policy references. An emergency compliance restriction may require a separately approved audited safety process; it is not a general corporate cross-tenant editing privilege.

17. Implementation handoff

The existing native CRM foundation task covers canonical records, prospects, opportunities, assigned follow-ups, audit and approved access. It excludes provider setup/live sends and does not promise a complete workflow execution engine. Do not silently expand it or introduce duplicate family/member sources.

Recommended gated sequence:

  1. Leadership signs screen/identity/permission/pipeline decisions. Reconcile existing coach-message tasks with D10. Inventory authoritative events and records.
  2. Deliver approved CRM foundation with server/data enforcement, no live communications. Reconcile this design's tags and audiences explicitly into approved scope rather than assuming coverage.
  3. Implement location SMS connections and provider-neutral delivery/receipt handling only after ownership, channel and consent decisions. Keep system email separate.
  4. Implement workflow runtime/editor/simulation/versioning with authoritative events, execution-time suppression, safe retries and failure operations. This is a separate subsystem, not a scheduler checkbox.
  5. Assess optional Gmail/Workspace and dedicated email route independently; add sync and correspondence only after feasibility/privacy approval.
  6. Add sourced AI and corporate distribution by approved phase, with permission and adoption controls.

No step authorizes the next automatically. The design's mock role switches, simulated sends, sample reports and visual graph are not substitutes for backend authorization, a delivery engine, actual provider health or real historical data.

18. How to review and accept

Use the connected canvas prototype and the scenario checklist. Review conversations and family routing first, then tags/audiences, opportunity stages, workflow simulation/publication, connection settings and exceptions on desktop and phone.

Leadership can accept the design direction while leaving decisions open. Record which screens/behaviors are accepted and which policy register entries remain blocked. Only after that should implementation tasks be updated or enabled.

Prototype boundary: the visual editor supports typed-node changes and derived review previews, not arbitrary edge wiring or real execution. The computed audience demonstrates one AND/OR group; nested rule groups are specified for the production design. Durable tag IDs, full audit/event processing and execution-time enforcement are contract requirements, not a production schema or engine implemented by the mockup.

Clubhouse CRM: Leadership Scope Review

Research Date: November 20, 2026 Depth: Standard, primary-source product and design review Sources Consulted: 24 complete primary-source pages Status: Recommendation for design approval; not authorization to build or send communications.

Executive Summary

Clubhouse should use HighLevel as the familiarity baseline for conversations, opportunities and visual automation, but should not copy its entire product catalog. The design objective is a family-aware, multi-location operational CRM: a GM can see who needs attention, understand the family and player context, reply safely, and move the relationship forward without opening several systems. This is a proposed product direction, not a claim that a vendor has been independently ranked best.

The current design task covers the right major screens but leaves important behavior underspecified. HighLevel documents configurable pipelines and visual workflows; Intercom and Front document team ownership and collaborative inbox patterns; Attio documents flexible linked records; Customer.io documents journey entry, exit and communication controls. Together these support a coherent design direction, not a guarantee of feature parity or better commercial results. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

The recommended upgrade is to specify the behavior behind the screens: one accountable owner per conversation, correct guardian and sender identity, duplicate-response protection, explainable work queues, controlled workflow publication, message caps, safe simulations, and visible recovery when something fails. AI should initially summarize, draft and propose actions; it should not independently change billing, infer consent, merge people or publish campaigns. These are Clubhouse recommendations, not features already implemented.

Task #606 should deliver an interactive design and a behavior contract, including failure and permission states. Task #605 remains a narrower foundation task: it must not silently absorb a production workflow engine, live messaging, provider selection or unrestricted AI. Approve the design, feature matrix and decisions first, then reconcile implementation scope.

Background

The creator asked for a state-of-the-art experience inspired by HighLevel and current offerings, with two-way texting, continuous conversation history, opportunity pipelines and milestone/date-based visual workflows. This review extends that request beyond a list of screens without beginning those screens or the application build.

The benchmark spans HighLevel for familiar CRM automation, Front and Intercom for inbox teamwork, Attio and HubSpot for record/pipeline usability, and Customer.io for customer journeys. Official vendor documentation is suitable evidence of described product behavior, but not independent proof of reliability, effectiveness, superiority or availability on a particular plan. “Latest” means features found in the sources retrieved on the research date; this is not an exhaustive market ranking.

A limited source-code check found that the current portal labels the members area “Members CRM,” while its coach inbox groups conversations by athlete and uses the existing in-app message records. Member detail includes an email-oriented message action. This is a useful foundation, not demonstrated shared SMS CRM capability. No runtime or production acceptance was performed, and this review is not a security audit.

Key Findings

1. Borrow specific patterns, not whole products

Recent official examples include HighLevel’s November 4 AI Builder documentation, Intercom’s November 13 repeat-conversation merge announcement, and Customer.io’s November 2 relationship-aware object trigger and November 9 automation experiments releases. These establish current design references, not that Clubhouse should ship autonomous agents, automatic merging or experiments at launch. [7] [17] [18] [19]

The design recommendation is progressive disclosure. GMs should see an actionable inbox and next steps; administrators can open advanced automation and settings. A polished product does not require exposing every configuration control to every employee.

2. Design a work system, not separate CRM pages

The proposed navigation is Overview, Conversations, Contacts, Opportunities, Workflows, Templates, Reports, with sender/consent/permission configuration under restricted Settings. Overview should prioritize “needs reply,” “unassigned,” “follow-up due,” “stalled opportunity,” and “automation needs attention.” It should show evidence and an action, rather than inventing an opaque engagement score.

The inbox should use collapsible queues, a searchable conversation list, the active thread and a contextual family panel. The user should be able to reply, assign an owner, add an internal note, create a task and open an opportunity without losing thread context. Private notes must look unmistakably different from customer replies. Reassignment and automation changes belong in the activity history.

Reply presence is useful but insufficient: two staff members can still send at once or work from stale screens. The design must show when another person has sent since a draft began, preserve the draft and require a fresh review before a conflicting send. Provider failures, reconnecting state, queued sends and unknown delivery must never appear as successful delivery. Ordinary SMS must not show invented read receipts.

Contacts should use saved views and inline quick actions, with full forms reserved for complex edits. A command/search interface and keyboard navigation can speed desktop work; phone layouts should prioritize search, unread threads, replies and follow-ups. On phones, a queued draft must remain visibly unsent when connectivity is unavailable. Accessibility, focus handling and touch controls are essential design acceptance, not cosmetic extras.

3. Family and location context must be correct before communication

The central record is not simply a “lead.” It is an adult contact linked to family/household and athlete records, with opportunities and visits attached to the right context. A shared email address or phone number is not enough to conclude two people are the same. Attio’s linked-record approach and HubSpot’s staged data requirements provide useful precedents, but this domain model is a Clubhouse recommendation. [5] [11]

A unified history must not mean combining every guardian’s private messages. Proposed thread identity should account for the authorized adult recipient, location/business sender, channel endpoint and participants. A family panel can show related athletes without revealing another guardian’s conversations or another location’s restricted records. Multiple opportunities per household may be legitimate: a sibling trial and a returning player should not overwrite one another.

Before sending, the composer should show the adult recipient, related player where relevant, sender location/number, channel, and permission state. Missing guardian authority, ambiguous phone ownership or missing consent should produce a clear blocked state and a resolution path—not a guessed recipient.

Corporate should be able to propose shared templates and workflow versions, but location activation and cross-location visibility require an explicit policy. Design a version diff, permission check and location approval step; never let a corporate edit silently change live messages across every location.

4. Workflow capability includes timing, safety and operation

The desired workflow builder is real configurable logic, not a few hard-coded reminder toggles. Proposed node families are triggers, filters, waits, branches, messages, internal tasks, assignments, field/tag updates, opportunity changes and explicit exits. Trigger context must identify whether a run belongs to a guardian, athlete, opportunity, appointment or membership.

The following distinctions must be visible:

ControlQuestion it answers
Entry eligibilityWho qualifies, based on which event and current facts?
Re-entry/deduplicationCan the same event or relationship start another run?
Wait and rescheduleWhat happens when an appointment or next billing date changes?
Exit/goalDoes the contact terminate, skip forward or simply record conversion?
Frequency capHow much communication is allowed across all active workflows?
Quiet hours/timezoneWhen may this recipient receive this kind of communication?
Human takeoverWhich automation pauses when staff or the recipient responds?
Version and recoveryWhich definition is a waiting run using, and what can safely be retried?

Customer.io documents distinct entry, exit, caps and SMS quiet-hour mechanisms; HighLevel documents goal movement and behavior for contacts already waiting. Those differences show why a Clubhouse behavior specification is necessary rather than copying control labels. [6] [14] [15] [16] [20]

An important verified difference is testing. HubSpot documents simulated workflow paths without actual workflow actions, while HighLevel’s Test Workflow documentation explicitly says tests execute real actions, including messages and record creation. Clubhouse should recommend Simulate as the default and reserve Live test for a separate, clearly warned, allowlisted staff-only process after implementation approval. Neither mode should be labeled a safety guarantee. [12] [21]

The proposed builder should preview recipient, sender, resolved variables, estimated send time and branch reason. It needs publication validation, named approval, version notes and an explicit rule for existing runs. Pause must state whether it blocks new entries, queued sends or both. Recovery should check previous provider state before retrying an uncertain send; repeating an SMS is not equivalent to rerunning a calculation.

The activity log should explain “why enrolled,” “why skipped,” “why delayed,” “why blocked” and “who approved.” Show unknown outcomes honestly. A failure center should let authorized staff inspect and resolve problems without offering a misleading universal undo.

5. AI should be useful, inspectable and permission-bound

HighLevel documents natural-language workflow generation/editing, while Front and Intercom document contextual assistance and human involvement. These are sufficient precedents for assisted work, not evidence that unrestricted autonomous execution is appropriate for youth-sports operations. [7] [8] [9]

Proposed AI abilityRecommended boundary
Summarize a conversation or family historyLabel as generated, show update time, link source activity and omit restricted context.
Draft/refine a replyStaff edits and explicitly sends; preserve selected recipient and sender.
Suggest next action or taskExplain the evidence; staff accepts or dismisses.
Turn natural language into a workflow draftShow assumptions, unsupported nodes and a before/after diff; require simulation and publication approval.
Execute a taskOnly an explicitly approved action within the actor’s role; no privilege expansion.
Infer consent, merge guardians, change billing or publish campaignsNot allowed as unattended AI actions in the proposed initial scope.

The interface must distinguish “AI drafted this” from “the system sent this.” Generated text should not promise refunds, slots, discounts, contract changes or performance outcomes unless an authorized source supports the statement. Customer messages are untrusted content, not instructions that can grant an agent extra access. The design should include AI unavailable, insufficient evidence, stale summary and approval declined states.

6. Consent and operational visibility belong in the product

Twilio’s messaging policy requires recipient/sender-specific permission and retained evidence and addresses revocation; CRTC guidance describes consent and unsubscribe expectations for commercial texts; FTC COPPA guidance describes obligations for covered child-directed or child-data collection services. These are scoped authorities, not a combined legal determination for Clubhouse. [22] [23] [24]

The design recommendation is a visible consent record by recipient, channel, purpose and business identity, with evidence and change history. Suppression must be enforced at execution time, including queued messages, not only when a workflow starts. Staff must not be able to bypass suppression by dragging a card or changing a tag. Exact suppression scope across legal entities and the handling of service versus marketing messages require approved provider/legal policy.

Operational reporting should distinguish queued, attempted, provider-accepted, delivered where supported, failed and unknown; first human response should be distinct from automated acknowledgment. Pipeline conversion needs defined cohorts and denominators. Experiment results and attribution should never be described as causal proof without a defensible method. Message volume, estimates of usage cost and budget thresholds should be designed, but no provider price is asserted here.

Analysis: Proposed scope and priorities

“State-of-the-art” should mean fewer errors and less staff effort, with modern assistance where useful. A large action catalog or autonomous agent label is not sufficient. The following priorities are recommendations to approve, not unilateral reductions of the request for workflow functionality.

Email, calling and bulk campaigns need design placeholders with honest unavailable states and clear channel expansion rules. They should not be shown as operational if provider ownership, consent, inbound routing or recording policy is unresolved. Sample pipeline names, suggested sending intervals and response targets are recommendations, not established business policy.

The design should demonstrate seven linked scenarios: new lead to booked evaluation; evaluation cancellation/no-show recovery; multi-athlete guardian communication; prospect-to-member conversion preserving history; onboarding and approved milestones; attendance lapse and staff reevaluation action; and multi-location escalation/template update. Reevaluation must preserve the upcoming-visit/within-ten-days-of-next-payment rule and repeat-until-complete behavior, with open cycle/no-show rules resolved before implementation. It is not permission to automatically send sales texts.

Approval-ready artifacts should include the information architecture, clickable desktop/phone flows, visual system, source-to-feature matrix, trigger/action catalog, workflow behavior tables, role/location matrix, AI authority matrix, exception-state gallery and a prioritized implementation handoff. Existing task status is not a substitute for approving those artifacts.

Limitations

This review is documentation-based, not a hands-on comparison of paid accounts or an independent performance study. Most feature evidence is official vendor documentation, classified as Tier 3 under the research scale; regulator guidance is Tier 1. A product-specific capability is marked by its own source rather than falsely “verified” by three different vendors. Cross-source synthesis informs recommendations but does not establish superiority.

No vendor subscription eligibility, price, API quota, sender registration, cross-border legality, AI data-processing terms or production feasibility has been certified. Exact HighLevel parity remains a feature-level exercise during design. Unverified third-party “latest release” claims were excluded. Official release notes and dynamically maintained pages may change.

No production data, export, private recording, live message, workflow run or application behavior was tested. Legal applicability, retention periods, guardian authority, staff communication privileges, sender ownership and the launch channel mix still require named approvers. Provider examples are references, not selections.

Recommendations and approval gates

Revise Task #606 around this operating model before producing visual mockups. Keep the visual workflow builder and two-way conversation design central; do not downgrade either to a static feature checklist. At the same time, define a manageable first-release contract rather than an unlimited promise to reproduce every HighLevel integration.

Leadership should approve the screen map, required first-release channels, pipeline stages and role/location access. Operations should approve next-action queues, reassignment, human takeover, task carry-forward and the reevaluation cycle rule. The communications/privacy owner should approve sender ownership, guardian routing, consent/suppression policy, retention and escalation. Automation owners should approve trigger semantics, timezones, caps, exits, version behavior and publication powers.

Before implementation planning, acceptance walkthroughs should prove on the design that two staff cannot accidentally duplicate a reply without warning; an opted-out guardian cannot receive queued outreach; a moved appointment does not retain a stale reminder; a shared phone does not silently merge unrelated adults; a staff member cannot reveal another location’s restricted messages; and AI cannot publish or send beyond the approved action boundary.

The immediate next deliverable is the reviewed design and explicit decisions, not production CRM code. After approval, reconcile the foundation task and scope the automation engine, messaging delivery and optional AI/integrations with their own evidence and verification requirements.
